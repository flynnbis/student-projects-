Introduction to hempcrete

1. What is hempcrete?

Hempcrete is a durable, lightweight and sustainable building material. It is made by mixing the inner core of the industrial hemp plant with lime binder and water. The material is a grey solid like concrete, with a rough texture. Typically, hempcrete would look like this:

2. Advantages

Hempcrete is made purely by natural resources. This means that it is eco-friendly and naturally fire and pest resistant. Also, the material is given the name "carbon negative" due to the fact that it absorbs all carbon dioxide when growing. This creates a net-negative carbon footprint if installed on walls, significantly improving the air quality and breathability. Furthermore, hempcrete is a excellent thermal regulator, meaning it can trap thermal energy in winter and let out excess in summer. Finally, hempcrete can regulate humidity naturally, which prevents mould and black rot

However, materials like traditional concrete are quite the opposite. They are not carbon negative, instead produces a large amount when being made, which significantly impacts the enviroment. Hempcrete would provide more advantages due to it being purely natural

3. Disadvantages

Though hempcrete does have its advantages, there are still limitations to consider for this material. Unlike concrete, hempcrete cannot support the weight of a building, but is used as an infill or requires alloy frames. Also, it is quite time consuming, as hempcrete can take up to weeks or months to fully dry. Hempcrete can also be quite expensive to make, and the material cannot be used without frames installed. Finally, mould will still form in hempcrete if it is wrapped in non-breathable paint.
