Arctic

The Arctic is a polar region located at the northernmost part of Earth, characterized by its cold climate, ice-covered landscapes, and unique wildlife.

Why is this environment extreme?

The Arctic is extreme due to its location at the poles, resulting in long periods of darkness and daylight, and extremely low temperatures.

Climate and Environment Details

Temperature and Precipitation

The Arctic experiences extremely low temperatures, often dropping below -40°C (-40°F). Precipitation is minimal, mostly in the form of snow.

Soil and Landscape

Arctic soils are typically frozen (permafrost) and have low organic matter. The landscape is dominated by ice, snow, and rocky terrain. Vegetation is sparse and mainly consists of mosses, lichens, and hardy shrubs.

Life in the Arctic

Animals in the Arctic are adapted to the cold environment through various mechanisms such as thick fur or feathers, layers of fat for insulation, and behavioral adaptations like hibernation or migration. Many species also have specialized kidneys to conserve water (e.g., polar bears, Arctic foxes, seals).

Suitable Materials for Building a Shelter

When building a shelter in the Arctic, the goal is to retain heat and protect against the cold. Suitable materials and approaches include:

  • Mud, adobe or rammed earth: good thermal mass that keeps interiors warm by absorbing heat during the day and releasing it at night.
  • Stone and rock: durable and provides thermal mass; often used for long-term structures.
  • Canvas, woven mats or shade cloth: lightweight options for temporary shelter; paired with insulation to reduce heat loss.
  • Insulating layers and reflective materials: reflective sheets or dark-coloured surfaces increase heat absorption.
  • Local vegetation and branches: used for simple shelter structures or windbreaks.

Design tips: orient entrances away from prevailing wind, use thick walls for thermal buffering, include ventilation for moisture control, and collect or store water if possible.

Human Adaptations and Survival tips

  • Wear multiple layers of clothing to trap heat and allow for ventilation.
  • Build or find shelter to protect from the elements.
  • Stay hydrated and ration activity to avoid peak heat.
  • Signal for help if in distress.
  • Carry navigation tools and signal devices; deserts can be vast and disorienting.

Conservation and Human Impact

Human activities such as climate change, pollution, and habitat destruction pose significant threats to the Arctic ecosystem. Conservation efforts focus on reducing carbon emissions, protecting critical habitats, and supporting indigenous communities who have lived in harmony with the Arctic environment for thousands of years.
