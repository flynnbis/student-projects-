Mycelium-Based

Mushrooms but with a doctorate in infarstructure.


Mycelium is the living body of a fungus. It produces hyphae, which is a type of hyphae, that spreads out in all directions in search of food. We can use its properties to make composite materials in the lab because when it grows, it latches on to it’s food like glue. This means as the fibres spread, they stick to particles like sawdust, straw, or other agricultural and industrial biomass waste, gluing’ them together. After a few days, this mixture becomes a lightweight but solid block. If we then stop the growth and eventually “kill” the fungi with heat, the block still holds its shape. The energy needed to make this is bare minimum, no machinery at all.
