Core Technology & Scientific Mechanisms

Bio-inspired structural glass seamlessly integrates molecular flexibility and high-stress physics, overcoming the major weaknesses of conventional tempered glass.

1. Buffer Layer (Ovalbumin + Amylopectin)

When ovalbumin proteins from egg whites combine with amylopectin from sticky rice, a flexible internal buffer layer forms. During impact, protein chains unfold and absorb kinetic energy while disulfide bonds strengthen the structure and reduce crack propagation.

2. Internal Core Skeleton (Prince Rupert's Drop)

Prince Rupert's Drops contain extremely high compressive stress on the outer surface. Applying this principle creates an invisible internal framework that improves impact resistance and allows the structure to withstand heavy loads.

Overcoming Material Limitations

Click each section below to reveal the engineering solutions used in the project.

Breakthrough 1 · Resolving Opacity (Ultrasonic Disintegration)
Uneven protein and starch particles scatter light and reduce transparency. High-frequency ultrasonic processing breaks these particles into nano-scale clusters smaller than visible light wavelengths, significantly improving optical clarity and transparency.
Breakthrough 2 · Preventing Degradation & Mold
Organic materials naturally absorb moisture and become vulnerable to microbial growth. Inspired by ancient sticky rice-lime mortar technology, calcium hydroxide is added to create antimicrobial protection and increase long-term durability.
